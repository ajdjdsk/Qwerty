# My Father's Son (Opus 4.8)

> **Направление / стиль:** ЭКСПЕРИМЕНТ С МУЖСКИМ ГОЛОСОМ (временно) — готик-симфо ядро; тёплый зрелый баритон/бас-баритон; приём «прорыв плотины» (зажатость → катарсис).

> **Смысл:** На похоронах отца сдержанный мужчина, которого с детства учили «мужчины не плачут», осознаёт, что стал точной копией своего молчаливого отца — те же руки, та же броня, тот же океан, что он держит в себе с девяти лет. И он решает разорвать эту цепь ради собственного сына: говорить «я люблю тебя», позволить мальчику увидеть, как он плачет. Песня о мужской эмоциональной броне, праве на слёзы и о том, как разорвать наследие молчания между поколениями. Близко очень многим. Эмоция: каменная сдержанность → прорыв плотины → освобождающие слёзы и нежная клятва.

## Lyrics

[Intro]
(a lone violin and piano, somber and spare; restrained, holding back)

[Verse 1 — clipped, restrained, near-spoken warm baritone, jaw tight]
They handed me the folded flag and shook my steady hand,
and I nodded like he taught me, jaw of stone;
he never said he loved me, but I came to understand
it was buried in the duty that he shown.

[Pre-Chorus — the orchestra tightens, low and tense]
'cause I've got his hands, his silence, got his hard and iron spine,
I've been holding back an ocean since I was nine —

[Chorus — a restrained swell, the dam still holding]
'cause the men don't cry, the men don't cry,
that's the only prayer my father ever taught;
so I'll carry it in granite, I will hold it till I die —
(but the granite's started cracking, and I never, never thought)

[Verse 2 — his own boy enters, fuller, cello deepening]
then my own son took my sleeve and said "Dad, it's alright to,"
and he looked at me with everything I hid;
forty years of swallowed water rising up to break on through,
and I saw the frightened, loving boy I was — the boy I'd been — the kid.

[Pre-Chorus — building hard, strings climbing]
and I thought of every "I'm fine," every door I closed too soon,
every time I taught this child to lock the room —

[Bridge — the DAM BREAKS; the held flood releases; voice cracks open into a soaring belt, full orchestra]
so I'm done, oh, I am done with holding back the flood,
let it come, let it come, let it break me on the floor,
let my boy see his father weeping over love and over blood,
'cause the strongest thing I'll ever do is finally feel it all — and more —

[Violin & Orchestra Flood]
(the violin soars and weeps over the full orchestra as the decades of restraint pour out at last)

[Final Chorus — the vow; tender major, soaring, cathartic]
so let the men cry, let the men cry,
let me break the iron prayer my father passed me down;
I will tell my son I love him every morning, every night,
I will let him see me crumble, I will let him see me drown —
and I'll hold him, oh, I'll hold him, while he's small enough to hold,
'cause the bravest men are tender, and the tender men are gold —
so let the men cry... let the men cry.

[Outro]
(the orchestra softens to the lone violin and piano; a man, at last, quietly weeping in the warmth)
(it's alright to... it's alright to...)
