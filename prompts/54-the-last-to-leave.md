# The Last to Leave (Opus 4.8)

> **Направление / стиль:** НОВОЕ НАПРАВЛЕНИЕ со всеми сильными сторонами ядра — средневековая/старинная музыка (ранняя готика: плэйнчант, органум, харди-гарди, виела) × полный готик-симфо оркестр и виртуозная скрипка. Приём: рост от монофонического чанта к полной полифонии; сквозной набат-колокол.

> **Смысл:** Чумной город, набат не смолкает над площадью; богачи и даже клир бегут в «чистые земли» — а простая женщина-целительница остаётся ходить за умирающими, потому что никто не должен уходить в одиночестве и страхе. Песня о священной смелости тех, кто остаётся у постели, когда весь мир бежит; о любви и долге в катастрофе; о вере, выраженной не уверенностью, а действием — держать руку каждого, за кем пришла смерть. Тема глубоко близка после пандемии и каждому, кто ухаживал за уходящими. Эмоция: суровый аскетичный чант → нарастающий ужас и решимость → возвышенная, самоотверженная клятва присутствия.

## Lyrics

[Intro]
(a single tolling plague-bell; a lone modal plainchant voice, monophonic and pure; a hurdy-gurdy drone enters beneath)

[Verse 1 — austere, chant-like, pure and restrained]
The carts go by at morning and the carts go by at night,
and the bell, the bell won't stop above the square;
they have bolted up the manor, they have run for cleaner light,
but the dying, oh, the dying are still there.

[Pre-Chorus — vielle and strings stir, the voice opening]
and I am only hands and water, only psalms I half-believe,
but I will not, I will not let them leave alone, and grieve —

[Chorus — soaring, organum choir and violin rising with the voice]
so let the bells ring, let them ring, I will stay, I will stay,
I will be the last to leave the dying door;
let the frightened keep their borders, let the holy run away,
I will hold the hand of every soul the plague has come for —
oh, let the bells ring, let them ring, I am here, I am here,
and not one, not one in my keeping will go alone in fear.

[Verse 2 — the toll mounts; her own fear; faith flickering, fuller]
I have buried more this fortnight than I christened in a year,
and the fever's at my temples, and I'm cold;
and I do not know what's waiting past the dark and past the bier,
if there's mercy, or just silence, in the fold.

[Pre-Chorus — building, psaltery and lute, organum thickening]
but a frightened child still reached for me at three o'clock today,
and I'll answer that with everything — let the doubting fall away —

[Bridge — the doubt resolved into action; presence as the prayer]
maybe there's no heaven, maybe no one's keeping score,
maybe all my whispered Latin's just a sound;
but a hand inside a dying hand is what the living's for,
and I'll be that, I'll be that, while the bells ring round —
(the choir swells) — let it be my one cathedral, let it be my creed:
to stay, to stay, to stay, with the ones the others leave —

[Hurdy-Gurdy & Violin Lift]
(the hurdy-gurdy drone, the organum choir and the solo violin climb into a towering polyphonic swell)

[Final Chorus — full polyphony, bells, choir and orchestra, belt with grit and grace]
so let the bells ring, let them ring, I have stayed, I have stayed,
I was the last to leave the dying door;
and if the dark should take me, I'll have lived the way I prayed —
with my hands in someone's hands as they were crossing to the shore —
oh, let the bells ring, let them ring, I was here, I was here,
and no one in my keeping ever crossed alone in fear.

[Outro]
(the polyphony falls away to the lone tolling bell and a single fading plainchant voice)
(I am here... I am here...)
