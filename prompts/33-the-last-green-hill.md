# The Last Green Hill (Opus 4.8)

> **Направление / стиль:** Кельтский плач (sean-nós / caoineadh) + кинематографичный готик-оркестр; uilleann pipes, модальный лад, 6/8.

> **Смысл:** Женщина навсегда покидает родную землю — с холодной переполненной палубы корабля она смотрит, как последний зелёный холм её родины складывается за горизонтом. Это не триумфальный побег, а вынужденный исход и щемящая тоска по дому, в который не вернуться. Но сквозь горе пробивается яростная нежная правда: «можно увезти девушку из долины, но нельзя увезти долину из девушки» — дом навсегда остаётся внутри. Тема близка любой диаспоре и каждому, кто покинул место, которого уже не вернуть. Эмоция: оголённый плач → общинный гимн стойкости → одинокое затихание над водой.

## Lyrics

[Intro]
(sea-wind and a lone gull; the uilleann pipes keen a modal melody with no words; a soft bodhran heartbeat; the fiddle answers low)

[Verse 1 — raw, low, ornamented sean-nos keening, just pipes and bodhran]
The deck is cold and crowded and the harbour's pulling thin,
and the only land I ever loved is folding itself in;
they say there's bread across the water, they say there's room to grow,
but they never say a word about the half of you you owe — to the leaving.

[Pre-Chorus — fiddle rises, low whistle, voice opening]
So I press my palm against the rail and hum the only prayer:
that the green will somehow follow me, that the hills will meet me there —

[Chorus — anthemic swell, fiddle and pipes soaring with the voice, belt with grace notes]
Goodbye to the last green hill, goodbye, goodbye,
to the rain on the gorse, to the turf-smoke and the sky;
you can take the girl from the valley, but you'll never, never still
take the valley from the girl — oh, goodbye to the last green hill.

[Verse 2 — fuller, bouzouki and harp enter, the lilt steady]
I have got my mother's locket and my grandmother's name,
a fistful of the churchyard soil to keep me near the same;
and the language that they'll laugh at on that far and crowded street
is the lullaby I'll whisper to the children at my feet.

[Pre-Chorus — building, the whole deck humming]
For a country isn't only on a map you leave behind,
it's the way you say "I love you," it's the shape of how you're kind —

[Chorus — bigger, gathering voices]
Goodbye to the last green hill, goodbye, goodbye,
to the rain on the gorse, to the turf-smoke and the sky;
you can take the girl from the valley, but you'll never, never still
take the valley from the girl — oh, goodbye to the last green hill.

[Bridge — the keen drops to lone pipes and bare voice; the shore vanishes; then a fierce, tender rise]
(almost a whisper)
There — it's gone now, just a thread of green against the grey,
and I'll never see it living, never walk that road in May...
(rising) but I'll build it in a kitchen, in a song, in a child's eyes,
I will plant the whole green valley underneath these foreign skies —

[Fiddle & Pipes Lift]
(the fiddle and uilleann pipes climb together into a soaring, communal lament-reel, the emigrant voices joining)

[Final Chorus — towering, full choir and orchestra, key lift, belt with tears]
So goodbye to the last green hill, but not, but not goodbye
to the hill I carry in me, that no ocean can deny;
you can scatter all my people from Donegal to here —
and we'll be the last green hill, we'll be the last green hill, my dear.

[Outro]
(everything falls away to the lone uilleann pipes and the sea-wind, the keening melody fading out over the water)
(goodbye... to the last green hill...)
