# Letters to the Girl I Was (Opus 4.8)

> **Смысл:** Зрелая женщина «пишет письмо» себе-маленькой — глядя на старую фотографию. Она хочет успокоить ту девочку: всё, чего ты боишься, тебя не убьёт; раны станут окнами, через которые войдёт свет; ты дойдёшь. И в конце — прощает себя прежнюю за наивность и ошибки. Песня о самопрощении и о том, что мы доживаем до версии себя, которой тогда так не хватало. Эмоция: тёплая нежность, перерастающая в сияющую силу; минор → мажор.

## Lyrics

[Intro — solo piano states the main theme, rich and purposeful, not tentative; a solo violin enters halfway with one long warm note above it; they breathe together before the voice arrives]

[Verse 1 — close, tender alto, long held notes gliding; piano leads under the voice the whole time while the violin weaves short warm answers and sustains beneath the phrases]
I found a photo with your crooked smile,
you're maybe ten, the whole world still a friend,
you don't yet know about the coming miles,
the doors that close, the loves that wouldn't mend.
You're squinting at a future you can't see,
so sure the worst of it would pass you by —
oh, if the lamplight only carried me
back through the glass to where you used to lie.

[Pre-Chorus — cello enters low, strings begin to stir, voice opens slowly and starts to swell, still controlled but the edge is showing]
'Cause I know every hour
that is going to break your heart,
and I'd trade my whole tomorrow
just to hold you in the dark.

[Chorus — full strings enter in waves, french horn warm and grand, piano driving, solo violin rising highest right alongside the voice (not just in the gaps); long melismatic belt swelling on every open vowel]
So this is a letter to the girl I was —
hold on, little one, you make it through;
the things you think will end you, oh, they don't, they only cause
the woman in the mirror who is so in love with you.
(oh-oh-ohh) don't you be afraid of all the falling,
every scar becomes a place the light gets in —
hold on, hold on, the girl I used to be,
I swear I made it home... and I came back for you again.

[Verse 2 — orchestra eases back but piano stays close and conversational under the voice, violin still threading beneath; vocal even more exposed and intimate]
You'll lose the ones you swore you'd always keep,
you'll learn that brave is mostly scared and staying,
you'll cry in cars and parking lots, and sleep
through mornings when you can't recall the praying.
But none of it, my darling, was the end,
not one of all the nights you thought you'd drown —
the very things you'll grieve will teach you how to mend,
and how to lay the heavy armor down.

[Pre-Chorus 2 — cello returns heavier, french horn enters early, strings more insistent, voice climbing hard and pouring]
So when they say you're "too much,"
when they leave you in the cold,
keep the fire that they ran from —
it's the truest thing you'll hold.

[Chorus — fuller than before, piano harder, violin at its highest, full orchestra, voice soaring with long held belts]
So this is a letter to the girl I was —
hold on, little one, you make it through;
the things you think will end you, oh, they don't, they only cause
the woman in the mirror who is so in love with you.
(oh-oh-ohh) don't you be afraid of all the falling,
every scar becomes a place the light gets in —
hold on, hold on, the girl I used to be,
I swear I made it home... and I came back for you again.

[Bridge — only piano and voice, the quietest and most intimate moment; piano simple and close, voice barely above a whisper with long rests between lines, like remembering slowly]
I used to hate you, little one,
for everything you didn't know,
for trusting hands that let me fall,
for being slow to let things go.
But I forgive you — oh, at last —
you did the best you could with fear;
and look — we're standing, we're still here,
and everything you dreamed of is so near.

[Final Chorus — maximum, key modulating fully into major, piano driving from the bridge, violin highest above everything, timpani and distant choir entering only now, voice cracking with emotion, melismatic on every open vowel]
So this is a letter to the girl I was —
you held on, little one, you brought us through;
the things you thought would end you, oh, they didn't, they became
the woman that you prayed for, and she's coming home to you.
(oh-oh-ohh) so don't you be afraid of all the falling,
every wound you carried is a window now, not sin —
hold on, hold on, the girl I used to be,
we made it, oh, we made it... let the rest of life begin.

[Outro — instruments fall away slowly; piano and violin remain longest playing together as they did in the intro, then piano fades to one hand, then a single warm violin note dissolving into light]
(hold on, little one... we made it through...)
