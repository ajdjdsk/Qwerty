# Dance, Old Friend (Opus 4.8)

> **Направление / стиль:** Готик театральное макабр-кабаре (danse macabre, cut-time gallop).

> **Смысл:** Женщина открывает дверь Смерти — не с ужасом, а с лукавой улыбкой, как давнему знакомому, который наконец зашёл. Вместо мольбы и слёз она зовёт его на последний танец и решает вести сама. Под остроумием — настоящая нежность к прожитой обычной жизни и спокойное, полное достоинства принятие конца. Отклонение по настроению: озорное, театрально-макабрическое, дерзко-весёлое и при этом тёплое. Живое, искрящееся, с подмигиванием.

Gothic theatrical chamber-orchestral, macabre cabaret, sly and darkly playful, mischievous and elegant — vividly alive and tongue-in-cheek. Warm mature female mezzo-soprano / contralto, smoky chest voice to a theatrical soaring belt, playful conversational phrasing with melismatic flourishes, sly ad-libs, breath and a knowing grin, intimate purr to full-throated. A lead solo violin trades licks with the voice continuously, gleeful and virtuosic; danse-macabre palette — galloping spiccato and pizzicato strings, harpsichord, skeletal xylophone, muted trumpet stabs, col legno taps, accordion, deep cello, harp, a delighted choir at the peak. Brisk cut-time gallop, film-score intensity, dynamic and surging (never martial), minor key with cheeky chromatics, rich reverb, full lively symphonic production, present and human. ‑lo-fi, ‑EDM, ‑trap, ‑autotune, ‑male lead, ‑monotone, ‑military march

## Lyrics

[Intro]
(a sly pizzicato gallop, harpsichord and a skeletal xylophone wink in, then the violin grins)

[Verse 1]
Well, look who finally came to call,
hat in your hand at my front door,
I always knew you'd crash the ball —
so wipe your boots upon my floor.
I'm not gonna scream, I'm not gonna run,
I've watched you take the best of them and lie;
pour yourself a glass, my patient one,
let's not waste a perfectly good goodbye.

[Pre-Chorus]
'Cause I've been brave and I've been tired,
and darling, you do dress to kill —

[Chorus]
So dance, old friend, go on and lead,
I'll follow every wicked little turn;
I won't go weeping, I won't plead,
I had my fire, and oh, I let it burn.
take my hand, you charming, awful thing,
and spin me till the candle's gone —
if this is the last good song you'll bring,
then play it, baby — let's dance on.

[Verse 2]
I've buried more than I'd confess,
I've kissed the warm ones and the cold,
I wore my sorrow like a velvet dress
and somehow got outrageously old.
so you don't frighten me, old crow,
I've grieved my own bright life in my own time;
I'll meet you grinning — off we go —
no, I won't beg, and I won't whine.

[Pre-Chorus]
'Cause I've been soft and I've been steel,
and you've been waiting, patient, still —

[Chorus]
So dance, old friend, go on and lead,
I'll follow every wicked little turn;
I won't go weeping, I won't plead,
I had my fire, and oh, I let it burn.
take my hand, you charming, awful thing,
and spin me till the candle's gone —
if this is the last good song you'll bring,
then play it, baby — let's dance on.

[Bridge]
(the gallop softens, suddenly sincere beneath the wit)
But between the jokes, I'll tell you true:
I loved it — every aching, ordinary day,
the burnt toast and the morning dew,
the people that I couldn't make to stay.
so I'm not afraid, I'm just... so full,
like a glass that spilled its whole bright wine —

[Violin & Harpsichord Solo]
(fiery, gleeful, the two chasing each other)

[Final Chorus]
(bigger, theatrical, a delighted choir like a clapping crowd)
So dance, old friend, go on and lead,
I'll match you, step for clever step;
I won't go bitter, I won't bleed,
I'll leave them laughing where I wept.
take my hand, you charming, awful thing,
and spin me till the morning's gone —
this is the last sweet song you'll bring,
so play it, baby — let's dance on.

[Outro]
(the gallop slows to one last sly pizzicato and a wink, a whispered line)
(go on, then... lead...)
