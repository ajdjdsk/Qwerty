# Carousel (Opus 4.8)

> **Направление / стиль:** Готик dark-carnival романтизм, кружащийся 6/8.

> **Смысл:** Жизнь как карусель, которую нельзя остановить и с которой нельзя сойти: расписные кони то взлетают, то падают (взлёты и потери), люди подсаживаются и сходят на остановках, которых ты не заметил, а музыка играет, готов ты или нет. Песня о головокружительной красоте и ужасе времени — и о том, что сама эта «поездка» и есть подарок. Эмоция: горько-сладкая, кружащаяся, нарастающе-динамичная (не боевая). Лёгкое тёмно-карнавальное звучание.

## Styles (≤ 1000)
Gothic cinematic orchestral, dark-carnival romanticism, emotionally dynamic and surging (never martial), bittersweet and spinning. Warm mature female mezzo-soprano / contralto, chest voice opening to a soaring melismatic belt, intimate to wall of sound, rubato glides and breath. A lead solo violin interweaves continuously with the vocal under and around every line; creative color instruments — a music-box and celesta motif, hammered dulcimer shimmer, swelling accordion, glockenspiel, harpsichord glints, galloping pizzicato strings in a lilting 6/8, deep cello, harp, pipe organ, soft timpani, distant choir at the peak. Virtuoso violin runs and double-stops, film-score intensity, dynamic ebb and surge, minor key with bittersweet major glints, rich cathedral reverb, full symphonic production, 6/8 compound meter, building momentum. ‑lo-fi, ‑EDM, ‑trap, ‑autotune, ‑male lead, ‑monotone, ‑pop brightness, ‑cheerful, ‑military march

## Lyrics

[Intro]
(music-box motif, then the violin lifts it up)

[Verse 1]
The painted horses rise and then they fall,
I climbed on young — I don't remember how,
the old calliope was playing for us all,
and I'm still reaching for the brass ring now.

[Pre-Chorus]
And the faces blur, oh, the ones who climbed beside me,
some got off at little stops I never got to see —

[Chorus]
Round and round on the carousel,
up with the laughter, down with the years,
I can't get off and I can't quite tell
if this dizzy spinning's joy or tears —
so hold the pole and ride it well,
'cause the music only plays so long;
round and round on the carousel,
oh, what a beautiful, terrible song.

[Verse 2]
I waved at my mother through the turning light,
I waved at my children as they grew and rode away,
the same brass horses, the same impossible flight,
the same sweet organ grinding night to day.

[Pre-Chorus]
And I used to fight it, oh, I used to scream to slow it,
now I throw my head back laughing — let it go, let it throw it —

[Chorus]
Round and round on the carousel,
up with the laughter, down with the years,
I can't get off and I can't quite tell
if this dizzy spinning's joy or tears —
so hold the pole and ride it well,
'cause the music only plays so long;
round and round on the carousel,
oh, what a beautiful, terrible song.

[Bridge]
(whispered, music-box alone)
And when the lights go dim and the gears at last run down,
and the last brave horse comes gently to a stop,
I won't ask for one more spin around this painted town —
I'll just whisper, "oh, thank you, for the whole wild lot."

[Violin & Hammered Dulcimer Solo]

[Final Chorus]
(key change, full and soaring)
So it's round and round on the carousel,
up with the living, down with the gone,
and I finally, finally learned to tell:
the spinning was the gift all along —
so ring the bell and ride it well,
let the dizzy, holy music carry on,
round and round on the carousel,
oh, what a beautiful, beautiful song.

[Outro]
(everything thins back to the music-box motif, one warm held note, then the gears slow to silence)
(round... and round...)
